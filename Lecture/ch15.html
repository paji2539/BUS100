Debt vs. equity: two ways to raise money

Beyond short-term needs, a growing business usually raises money one of two fundamental ways.

Debt financing

The accounting equation
Assets = Liabilities + Owners’ Equity. Everything a company owns (assets) was paid for either by borrowing (liabilities) or by the owners’ own investment (equity) — the equation always has to balance.

Special focus

Debt vs. equity financing

When a company needs to raise real money, it almost always comes down to this choice — and the two paths trade off very differently.

Debt financing

Borrow it

The company borrows money and must repay it, usually with interest, on a set schedule — regardless of how the business performs.

The lender has no ownership claim, so the founder keeps full control — but repayment is required even in a bad year.
Equity financing

Sell a piece of it

The company sells ownership shares to investors in exchange for capital — no repayment obligation, but the founder gives up some ownership and control.

Investors share the risk with the company — if the business struggles, they don’t have to be repaid on a fixed schedule.
