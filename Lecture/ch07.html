Objective 7.3

Why organization design matters

Once you know what needs doing, you need a structure that says who reports to whom.

Organizing
Arranging resources and people so the organization can achieve its goals.
Organizational chart
A visual diagram showing authority and reporting relationships within a company.

Two classic shapes

Vertical (tall) structure
tap to flip
Many layers of management between the top and the front line — narrow spans of control, clear chain of command.
Horizontal (flat) structure
tap to flip
Few layers of management, wider spans of control, faster decisions but more responsibility per manager.

Other structures worth knowing

📊
Matrix
Employees report to two managers at once — a functional manager and a project manager.
🔄
Inverted
Puts frontline, customer-facing employees at the top, with management structured to support them.
🌐
Network
A core company outsources most functions and coordinates a web of outside partners.
Objective 7.4

Keeping the business on track

Plans rarely survive contact with reality untouched — controlling is how managers catch that early.

Controlling
Measuring performance against goals and correcting problems as they arise.

The control cycle

1

2
Measure performance
3
Compare results
4
Correct problems

Quality management approaches

TQM
Total Quality Management · tap to flip
A company-wide commitment to continuously improving quality in every process, not just the final product.
Six Sigma
tap to flip
A data-driven method for reducing defects and variation in a process to near-zero error rates.
Real-world example
Nike stopped working with vendors that used child labor — which means it now runs independent audits of its suppliers on a regular basis, a direct example of the control cycle applied to ethical compliance.
