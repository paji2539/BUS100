Objective 2.1

Two ways to look at the same economy

Economics splits into two studies that ask different questions at different altitudes. Tap each to open it up.

The study of how individuals and businesses make decisions to best satisfy wants, needs, and desires with limited resources. Scarcity is the whole reason the field exists — if resources were unlimited, there would be nothing to decide.

The study of how individual businesses, households, and consumers decide to allocate their limited resources in the exchange of goods and services. Should we raise our price? How many units do we make?

The study of the behavior of the overall economy. Is it growing or shrinking? Are prices rising? Are people employed? Is borrowing cheap right now?

Objective 2.2

Supply and demand

Supply is the quantity of goods or services made available for sale. Demand is the quantity of a good or service people want to buy at any given time. They respond to price in opposite directions — which is exactly why a price ever settles anywhere.

Law of supplyPrice ↑ → supply ↑ · Price ↓ → supply ↓
Law of demandPrice ↓ → demand ↑ · Price ↑ → demand ↓

Drag the price and watch what happens to each side. The point where the two lines cross is the market price, also called the equilibrium price.

Objective 2.5

The business cycle

The business cycle is the periodic increase and decrease of economic output. It runs on its own — and the government has two sets of levers for smoothing out the swings.

Managed by the federal government using two tools: tax policy, which changes how much money stays in people’s hands to spend, and government spending, which puts money into the economy directly and creates jobs along the way.

Managed by the Fed and aimed at the money supply — the combined amount of money available within an economy. The Fed works through open market operations, the discount rate, and the reserve requirement.
